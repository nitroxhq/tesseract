how is tesseract different from other, more specialized productivity tools, such as Notion, Todoist, Rize, Anki, etc?

tesseract's holistic approach to these and other app categories as well as its object-based and file-first structure is the foundation of its user-centric deign, most notably by:
- clears the clutter of often rigid app-first logic,
- the limited scope or higher mental workload/starting cost to use these tools most effectively,
- and the vendor lock-in of an app-centric data storage methodology as opposed to being file-first.

how does tesseract plan to make money?

nitrox plans to monetize tesseract by reserving collaboration for large teams and usage of our own cloud servers for storage and sync for paying organizations and customers.

are there any limitations on the main (free) version?

all critical tesseract functionality is free for everyone. and there's no vendor lock-in, as all your data is stored as file types, not a black box of data on a server somewhere. however, collaborative and cloud services (only when hosted on nitrox's servers) by nitrox are paid in order to pay for server cost. Furthermore, users of such features are usually large organizations or customers with the means to pay for such features.

is my data private?

your data in tesseract and any nitrox app will never be sold to data brokers. we have no access to your data, as tesseract will rely on users to manage storage and sync of its content.
